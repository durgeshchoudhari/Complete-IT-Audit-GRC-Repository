# Complete Audit Lifecycle

## 1. Audit universe and annual planning
Inventory auditable entities, applications, processes, locations, vendors, and change initiatives. Score risk using factors such as financial impact, data sensitivity, regulatory exposure, complexity, prior findings, change, incidents, and management concern. Use professional judgment and document why an area is included or deferred.

## 2. Engagement initiation
Confirm mandate, objective, scope, period, stakeholders, reporting line, criteria, resources, timing, confidentiality, and independence. Hold an opening meeting.

## 3. Preliminary understanding and risk assessment
Review policies, process maps, prior reports, incidents, system architecture, data flows, contracts, regulatory obligations, and management reports. Conduct interviews and walkthroughs. Identify inherent risks and relevant controls.

## 4. Audit planning
Define audit objectives, scope boundaries, criteria, key risks, control objectives, procedures, sampling approach, evidence needs, and workpaper responsibilities. Prepare an audit program and request list.

## 5. Walkthrough
Trace one or more transactions end-to-end from initiation to recording/reporting. Identify people, systems, interfaces, approvals, automated controls, handoffs, and evidence. A walkthrough supports process understanding and design assessment; it is not automatically sufficient operating-effectiveness testing.

## 6. Design effectiveness
Determine whether the control as designed can address the identified risk. Evaluate precision, frequency, owner competence, segregation of duties, information used, escalation, and evidence retention.

## 7. Operating effectiveness
Obtain a complete population for the audit period. Validate source, parameters, date range, record count, filters, and reconciliation. Select samples using a documented rationale. Inspect, observe, inquire, reperform, or use analytics as appropriate. Record evidence and results for each sample.

## 8. Evaluate exceptions
Confirm the criteria, facts, timing, root cause, frequency, population impact, compensating controls, and business impact. Expand testing where warranted. Discuss factual accuracy with the owner. Do not jump from a data flag to a finding.

## 9. Communicate results
Draft findings with criteria, condition, cause, effect/risk, recommendation, management response, owner, and target date. Validate facts and rate issues using approved methodology.

## 10. Reporting and follow-up
Issue the report to intended recipients. Track actions, validate closure evidence, retest where needed, and escalate overdue or ineffective remediation.

## Core evidence methods
- Inspection: examine tickets, approvals, logs, reports, contracts, and configurations.
- Observation: watch a control being performed.
- Inquiry: ask responsible personnel; corroborate with evidence.
- Reperformance: independently execute the control or calculation.
- Confirmation: obtain information from an independent party.
- Data analytics: test a population or identify unusual items; validate data reliability.

## Sampling
Define population, unit, period, source, completeness/accuracy checks, sample method, sample size rationale, and treatment of deviations. Sample size depends on risk, control frequency, expected deviation, reliance, and methodology. Do not invent a universal sample size.
