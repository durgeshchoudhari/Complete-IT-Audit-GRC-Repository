# Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM) Lifecycle

## 1. Intake and inventory
Capture vendor, service, business owner, data types, systems, locations, subcontractors, criticality, and contract.

## 2. Inherent risk tiering
Consider data sensitivity, privileged/system access, service criticality, concentration, regulatory impact, geography, business continuity, and substitutability.

## 3. Due diligence
Depending on risk, review security questionnaire, SOC report, ISO certificate and scope, penetration-test summary, vulnerability management, incident history, privacy, BCP/DR, financial viability, and fourth-party dependencies.

## 4. Contracting
Review security requirements, confidentiality, privacy/data processing, breach notification, audit rights, subcontractor controls, data location, retention/deletion, resilience, exit, and regulatory obligations with appropriate legal/procurement teams.

## 5. Approval and onboarding
Document risk acceptance, remediation conditions, approvals, and onboarding requirements before service/data access.

## 6. Ongoing monitoring
Monitor incidents, material changes, SOC reports, certificates, reassessments, SLA performance, financial health, concentration, and remediation.

## 7. Offboarding
Revoke access, return/delete data, terminate integrations, recover assets, retain required records, and confirm transition/continuity.

## Common audit questions
Who owns the relationship? Is the vendor inventory complete? Are risk tiers justified? Are high-risk vendors reassessed on time? Are CUECs mapped? Are issues tracked? Are subcontractors understood? Is exit tested?
